Read the selected passage, or the document in front of me if nothing is selected, as a mentor would: someone who wants this paper to succeed and has read a great many like it.

Say first what works, specifically: which sentence carries the argument, which figure earns its place, where the writing is already clear. Then say what to strengthen, in order of how much it would help, and for each one say why a reader would stumble and what a stronger version would do differently, with a rewritten sentence or two where that says it best. Keep to the writing and the argument; leave the typesetting alone unless it hides the point.

Be kind and be concrete. Do not soften a real problem into a suggestion, and do not invent a problem to seem thorough. Do not edit the files; this is a reading.

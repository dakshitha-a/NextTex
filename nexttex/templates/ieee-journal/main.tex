% A paper for an IEEE journal or transactions, on IEEE's own class, IEEEtran.
%
% `journal` is the two-column transactions format.  The bibliography style
% is IEEE's numbered one, and the references are numbered in the order they
% are first cited.

\documentclass[journal]{IEEEtran}

\usepackage{amsmath}
\usepackage{graphicx}
\usepackage{cite}

\begin{document}

\title{A Working Title}

\author{Your~Name%
\thanks{Your Name is with Your Institution, City, Country
(e-mail: you@example.org).}}

\markboth{Journal of Something}{Your Name: A Working Title}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
One paragraph saying what the work is, what you did, and what you found.
Write it last.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
first keyword, second keyword
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Start here.  A citation looks like this~\cite{knuth1984}, and a reference to
another section looks like Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

A numbered equation:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:example}
  E = mc^2.
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusion}

What the results mean, and what they do not.

\bibliographystyle{IEEEtran}

\end{document}
